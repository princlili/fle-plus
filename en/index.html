The Question

What can a language offer someone who doesn't love it yet?

Not "you'll need French later." That doesn't work. Humans are moved by what matters to them now — by real problems, real stakes, real conversations.

FLE+ starts from a different place: what do you care about most, as a human being? And can you begin to think, build, or create something around that — in a language you barely master?

This is not a school exercise. It is a practice of transformation.

The Approach

FLE+ creates conditions where French becomes necessary — not because it is imposed, but because it is the only available space for a conversation that matters.

Learners — in classrooms, workshops, or other contexts — are invited to identify what they genuinely care about and to use French as the medium for developing a real project around it. The project can take any form: analog, digital, or hybrid. What matters is that it is theirs.

The teacher does not deliver content. The teacher builds a framework — a situation, a challenge, a set of stakes — and then steps back. Learners appropriate that framework in small groups. French becomes the working language of something that already has meaning.

Mistakes are part of the process. Levels (A1, B2...) are a rough map, not the territory. What matters is whether a learner can do something real with what they have — even imperfectly, even partially.

Principles in Progress

Genuine collaboration

Not simulated. Learners need each other to move forward. Group work is not a pedagogical technique here — it is the condition under which the project exists at all.

Long-term projects rooted in real challenges

A single lesson changes nothing. FLE+ works over time, with projects that grow, fail, get revised, and eventually produce something — an object, a conversation, a prototype, a text. Something that didn't exist before.

Usefulness beyond the grade

Even learners who will never love French can find something useful here, if the project touches something they actually care about. The goal is not to produce enthusiasm for the language. The goal is to make the language a means — to something larger.

Openness to all forms

Digital, analog, performative, written, spoken — FLE+ does not privilege one format. The project takes the form that fits the people making it.

Documented observation

This approach is built from watching real learners in real situations, not from theory applied downward. What is documented here (anonymized) is what actually happened — including what didn't work.

Contribute

FLE+ is an open project. It builds slowly, from the ground up.

If you teach French — or any language — in any context, and you recognize something here: you are welcome. Share an observation, a framework, a failure. Open an issue. Fork the repository. Write.
