The Challenge

Too often, learners study French without understanding why. Result: demotivation, mechanical learning, dropout.

The central question: How do we create meaning when it doesn't exist at the start?

The Vision

What if French were not an end in itself, but a tool for development? FLE+ proposes to transform French as a Foreign Language teaching by going beyond simple linguistic mastery.

Our conviction: French can become a catalyst for developing essential transversal competencies: critical thinking, resilience, collaboration, creativity.

Key Principles

  • Learner-centered: Students actively construct their knowledge
  • Group work: Collaborative learning as the norm
  • Oral priority: Spoken interaction as the main modality
  • Appropriable fictional frameworks: The teacher builds worlds that learners appropriate in groups, creating a space to deepen and question reality
  • Open architecture: A collaborative model inspired by Linux

The 4 Learning Pathways

What can French teaching really be used for? We propose 4 complementary pathways that learners can explore according to their needs:

1. Tool for Metalinguistic Awareness

Better understand one's mother tongue and other languages through comparison. Explore how each language structures thought differently.

2. Tool for Personal Development

Develop tolerance for ambiguity, resilience in the face of imperfection, and confidence in adversity. French as a laboratory for overcoming fear of judgment.

3. Communication Tool

Master communicative skills to interact effectively in French, according to the traditional approach but reinvented.

4. Tool for Intercultural Openness

Discover other ways of seeing and understanding the world. Develop the ability to navigate between cultures, recognize one's own frames of reference, and open up to other perspectives. French as a bridge to otherness.

The Method

The teacher creates a fictional framework (situation, resources, instructions).

Learners appropriate this framework in groups, explore it, transform it.

Through this fictional space, transversal competencies emerge naturally while they use French to act.

Motivation doesn't come from an explanation ("French is useful") but from an experience ("I was able to do something that matters to me").
