The Challenge

We teach French as a tool for the future: "You'll need it later, for travel, for work..."

But humans aren't motivated by abstract futures. They're motivated by present urgency, by immediate need.

The fundamental question: How do we make French a need now, as essential as eating or drinking?

The Vision

What if French were not a tool for thinking, but the very space where certain thoughts become possible?

French becomes fundamental when it becomes the only way to respond to real, immediate needs:

  • The need to be understood about something urgent
  • The need to belong to a group, a conversation
  • The need to explore existential questions
  • The need to be a version of oneself that can't exist elsewhere

Our conviction: French becomes essential when it becomes the unique space where we can question our place in the world. Not tomorrow. Now.

Key Principles

  • Learner-centered: Students actively construct their knowledge
  • Group work: Collaborative learning as the norm
  • Oral priority: Spoken interaction as the main modality
  • Appropriable fictional frameworks: The teacher builds worlds that learners appropriate in groups, creating a space to deepen and question reality
  • Open architecture: A collaborative model inspired by Linux

The 4 Dimensions of Need

When French becomes the space for essential questioning, four dimensions of fundamental human needs reveal themselves:

1. The need to understand how we think

Discover that one's mother tongue structures thought in a certain way. Feel viscerally that French opens other pathways of thought. Metalinguistic awareness emerges from lived contrast, not taught.

2. The need to grow in the face of uncertainty

Dare to speak despite imperfection. Tolerate ambiguity. Develop resilience. French as a space where one can fail without breaking, where one can be vulnerable without danger.

3. The need to be understood and to understand

Communicate truly, not recite. Exchange about what matters. French becomes the means to connect authentically with other humans, here, now.

4. The need to open up to otherness

Step outside one's frames of reference. Discover that others see the world differently. French as a bridge to other ways of being and understanding.

The Method

The teacher creates a fictional framework: a situation, questions, stakes that matter.

Learners appropriate this framework in groups. French becomes the only space where these questions can be explored, where these conversations can happen.

Urgency emerges naturally: "I need French now to say something essential."

Not "French will be useful later," but "French is necessary here and now for this conversation, this question, this understanding."

Transversal competencies—critical thinking, collaboration, resilience, creativity—are not objectives but natural consequences of authentic engagement in questions that matter.
